# DEMO ONLY — fictional guide: how to complain or raise an issue

This invented test content describes a pretend complaint process at the fictional Example Shop. It is not a real retailer's complaint policy or service commitment.

## How to raise an issue

Tell the assistant what went wrong in your own words. The assistant will check what kind of problem it is, ask for any missing details, and confirm with you before raising a support ticket. You can also ask to speak to a member of the support team at any time.

## What to include

To help us resolve your issue quickly, please share:

- Your order ID, in the format ORD-1234.
- Which item is affected, if your order had more than one.
- What happened, in a few sentences.
- When you noticed the problem, for example on the day of delivery.
- What you would like us to do, such as a replacement, return or refund.

## Damaged or defective items

If an item arrived broken, cracked or not working out of the box, tell us as soon as possible after delivery. Describe what is damaged and keep the item and all original packaging. Do not throw away the box, as it may be needed for a pickup or inspection. Exchanges for damaged or defective items can be requested within 10 days of delivery in this fictional example. Faults that appear later may be covered by the product warranty.

## Wrong or missing items

If you received the wrong product, a different colour or model, or an item is missing from your parcel, tell us the order ID and what you expected versus what you received. If your whole order shows as delivered but you did not receive it, tell us the order ID and any details, such as whether a neighbour or security desk may have accepted it. We will look into the delivery; a parcel is not declared lost until it has been investigated.

## Photo evidence

Photos help the support team review your issue faster. Where possible, take clear pictures of:

- The damaged or wrong item.
- The outer box and shipping label.
- Any damage to the packaging.

The support team may ask you to share these when they review your ticket.

## Ticket numbers

When a ticket is raised, you receive a ticket number in the format TCK- followed by eight letters or digits, for example TCK-1A2B3C4D. You can follow its progress in the My Tickets tab. Please quote your ticket number whenever you contact us about the same issue, so we do not open a duplicate.

## Response times and priority

In this fictional example, the support desk is open Monday through Friday from 09:00 to 17:00 local time. Tickets for damaged items and missing deliveries are treated as high priority and reviewed first. Other tickets, such as return requests, exchange requests and general requests to speak to the team, are reviewed in order. This demo does not promise a specific reply time.

## Escalation

If your issue is not resolved, or you are not happy with the answer, reply on the same conversation and ask to speak to a member of the support team. Mention your existing ticket number and explain what is still unresolved. The assistant will pass your conversation summary to the team so you do not need to repeat everything.
